## Don't Say S

**1 line description** → A fast word association game where saying any word that starts with "S" costs you a drink.

**When to play? (Emotional Outcome)** → When you want quick laughs and a bit of panic. Great as a warm-up to get everyone talking and slipping up early.

**Number of people required** → 3 or more

**How to Play** →

1. Sit in a circle. The first player says any word to start.
2. The next player says a word linked to the previous word.
3. Keep going around the circle as fast as you can.
4. If a player says a word starting with S, they drink.
5. If a player hesitates too long or repeats a word, they drink.
6. After a slip, that player starts a new chain with a fresh word.

**Rules** →

- No words starting with S, ever.
- The word must connect to the previous one. The group can challenge a weak link.
- Example: "Ocean" → "Wave" → "Surf" ❌ (drink).

## Truth or Drink

**1 line description** → Answer the question honestly, or take a drink to skip it.

**When to play? (Emotional Outcome)** → When you want the group to feel closer, exposed and a little scandalous. Best mid to late evening once people are comfortable.

**Number of people required** → 2 or more

**How to Play** →

1. Pick who goes first. They ask the person on their left a question.
2. That person chooses: answer truthfully, or drink instead.
3. The person who just answered (or drank) asks the next question to the person on their left.
4. Keep going around the circle.

**Rules** →

- If you answer, it must be the truth.
- No pressing for more details once someone chooses to drink.
- Example: "Who here would you least want to be stuck on an island with?"

## Most Likely To

**1 line description** → Everyone points at the person most likely to do something, and whoever gets the most fingers drinks.

**When to play? (Emotional Outcome)** → When you want playful roasting and big reactions. Great for groups who know each other well.

**Number of people required** → 4 or more

**How to Play** →

1. Someone reads out "Name somebody at the table who is most likely to…" (e.g. "…text their ex tonight").
2. Count down 3, 2, 1. Everyone points at one person at the same time.
3. The person with the most fingers pointed at them drinks.
4. If there is a tie, everyone tied drinks.
5. The next player reads the next prompt.

**Rules** →

- You must point at someone at the table.
- Everyone points at the same time, on 1.
- Example: "Most likely to fall asleep at the party."

## Spell B (Spelling Bee)

**1 line description** → A drunken spelling bee played on the site: listen to the word, type it out, and do a drinking dare if you get it wrong.

**When to play? (Emotional Outcome)** → When you want silly, competitive fun that gets harder (and funnier) as the night goes on.

**Number of people required** → 2 or more (no host needed, because the site reads the words)

**How to Play** →

1. Pick a level on the site: Easy, Medium or Hard.
2. Pass the phone to the first player. The site reads their word out loud.
3. The player types the spelling on the site and taps **Check**.
4. The site says if it's right or wrong.
5. If it's right, pass the phone to the next player for a new word.
6. If it's wrong, the site gives the player a drinking dare to do, then the phone moves on.

**Rules** →

- You can replay the word as many times as you like, but no peeking at anyone's phone for the spelling.
- Once you tap **Check**, your answer is final.
- Wrong spelling means you do the dare on screen, no arguing.
- Non-drinkers can swap any sip for a sip of water.
- Example words: "necessary", "rhythm", "conscientious".

### How It Works in the App

*Built. Play it from the games page (Spell B). Players enter their names first, then pick a level.*

**1. Pick a level** → The player chooses Easy, Medium or Hard.

**2. Listen** → The site reads the word out loud. A **🔊 Hear it again** button replays it as many times as needed. Below it, the player also sees:

- **In a sentence:** an example sentence, with the word blanked out ("____") so it doesn't give the spelling away.

**3. Type it** → The player types the spelling into a box and taps **Check**.

**4. The site checks it** →

- **Right:** "✅ Correct!" shows, with a **Next** button for the next player.

**5. End of round** → If most of the answers in the round were right, the site asks whether the players want to move up a level, with **Yes** and **No** buttons. Yes switches to the next harder level, and No keeps the same level.

**Decisions made when we built it** →

- Every word in the Word Bank has a meaning and an example sentence.
- The end of each round shows each player's score (words right out of words played).

## He's a 10 but…

**1 line description** → One player guesses the number on their forehead from everyone's "He's a 10 but…" clues.

**When to play? (Emotional Outcome)** → When you want creative, laugh-out-loud moments and hot takes on dating red flags.

**Number of people required** → 3 or more (1 guesser + 2 or more clue-givers)

**How to Play** →

1. The guesser holds a phone up to their forehead without looking. A number from 1–10 shows to the rest of the group.
2. The other players take turns saying "He's a 10 but…" followed by a trait that should bring him down to that number.
3. After each clue, the guesser makes a guess.
4. If the guess is right, the guesser wins the round and the turn passes to the next person with a new number.
5. If the guess is wrong, the person who gave that clue drinks, and the next person gives the next clue.

**Rules** →

- Clue-givers can't say the number or hint at it with fingers.
- The guesser can't look at the screen.
- Example: Number is 2 → "He's a 10 but he chews with his mouth open and calls his mum during dates."

## 7-11

**1 line description** → A counting game to 21 where numbers get swapped for new rules, and every mistake means a drink and starting over.

**When to play? (Emotional Outcome)** → When you want chaotic focus and loud group celebrations. Gets funnier as the rules stack up.

**Number of people required** → 3 or more

**How to Play** →

1. Sit in a circle and count from 1 to 21, one number per person.
2. Starting rule: 7 and 11 are swapped. Say "11" when it's 7, and "7" when it's 11.
3. If someone makes a mistake, they drink and the next person starts again from 1.
4. If the group reaches 21 with no mistakes, everyone drinks.
5. The person who said 21 adds a new rule for another number (swap it with a different number, clap instead of saying it, say a word, dance, or any action).
6. Start again from 1 with all the rules in play.

**Rules** →

- Rules stack. Every new rule stays for the rest of the game.
- Any mistake (wrong number, forgotten action, too slow) means a drink and a restart from 1.
- Example new rule: "Instead of saying 4, you clap twice."

### How It Works in the App

*Partly built. The app already has a 7-11 Rule Shuffle screen, opened from the games page, that draws a random rule with a shuffle animation. The "Hit 21!!" button, the pop-up and the "Go fish for a rule" flow below aren't built yet.*

**1. Count to 21** → The group counts around the circle as usual, with the host holding the phone.

**2. Hit 21!!** → When the group reaches 21 without a mistake, the host taps a big **Hit 21!!** button on the screen.

**3. Celebration pop-up** → A pop-up appears: "Congratulations! Now everyone drinks a sip 🍻".

**4. New rule** → After the pop-up, the screen shows the rule: "The person who said 21 gets to make a new rule."

**5. Go fish for a rule** → At the bottom of the screen is a button, **🎣 Go fish for a rule!** (or "Shuffle to pick a rule"), for when the player can't think of one. Tapping it plays a rotating, flipping card animation that slows down and lands on one rule from the Rule Shuffle list below.

**To decide when we build it** →

- The button label: "Go fish for a rule!" or "Shuffle to pick a rule".
- How the pop-up closes: an **OK** button like Spell B, or it closes by itself after a few seconds.
- Whether the app keeps a list of the rules in play, so the group can check them mid-game.
- Whether there's also a button for mistakes ("Someone messed up! Drink and restart from 1").

## Ick or Not

**1 line description** → Someone names a habit, everyone votes "ick" or "not", and everyone who votes ick drinks.

**When to play? (Emotional Outcome)** → When you want lively debates and to find out everyone's weird pet peeves. Great for chatty groups.

**Number of people required** → 3 or more

**How to Play** →

1. A player reads out a habit or trait (e.g. "wears socks with sandals").
2. Count down 3, 2, 1. Everyone votes at the same time: thumbs down for "ick", thumbs up for "not".
3. Everyone who voted "ick" drinks.
4. The next player reads the next one.

**Rules** →

- Everyone votes at the same time. No changing your vote.
- Defending your vote is encouraged.
- Example: "Says 'literally' in every sentence."

### How It Works in the Website

*Built. Play it from the games page (Ick or Not).*

**1. Rules first** → The game opens on the rules screen (description, players needed, How to Play and Rules), with a **Let's play** button.

**2. Trait on screen** → The site shows one trait from the Question Bank in big text, with its category (for example "Dating 💔"). Someone reads it out loud.

**3. Ready to vote** → When everyone has made up their mind, someone taps **Ready to vote**.

**4. Countdown** → A countdown runs on screen: 3, 2, 1, **GO!**

**5. Vote** → At GO, everyone votes at the same time with their hand: 👍 for "Not" or 👎 for "Ick".

**6. Drink** → The site says: "Everyone who voted 👎 Ick, drink! 🍺", with a **Next trait** button.

**7. Next trait** → Tapping **Next trait** shows a new trait, and the game goes back to step 2. Traits don't repeat until the whole bank has been used.

**To decide when we build it** →

- Whether the group can choose categories before starting (Dating, Habits & Manners, Online & Phone, Style & Hygiene), like the 7-11 category filters.
- Whether a game ends after a set number of traits (for example 20) or keeps going until the group stops.
- Whether a unanimous vote gets a twist (for example, if everyone votes Ick, the person who read it out drinks too).
- Whether the site keeps a tally of Ick votes, so it can show the "biggest ick of the night" at the end.

## Name Somebody at This Table Who…

**1 line description** → A whispered question, a name said out loud, and a coin flip decides if the question gets revealed.

**When to play? (Emotional Outcome)** → When you want suspense, paranoia and a lot of "wait, what was the question?!". Best when the group is comfortable.

**Number of people required** → 4 or more

**How to Play** →

1. A player whispers a question to the person next to them: "Name somebody at this table who…" (e.g. "…would be the worst roommate").
2. That second person says their answer out loud to the table. The answer must be someone at the table.
3. Flip a coin.
4. If it lands on heads, the question is announced to everyone.
5. If it lands on tails, the question stays secret.
6. The second person can choose to drink instead of having the question announced.
7. The second person then whispers a new question to the next person.

**Rules** →

- The answer must be someone sitting at the table.
- Only the coin (or a drink) decides if the question is revealed.
- Example: "Name somebody at this table who you'd call at 3am."

### How It Works in the App

**2. Question** → The first question pops up on the screen ("Name somebody at this table who…"), with a coin at the bottom. The person holding the phone whispers it to the person next to them, who says their answer out loud.

**3. Flip the coin** → Once the question is answered, the person holding the phone taps the coin. It plays a rotating, flipping animation and lands on **Heads** or **Tails**.

**4. Result pop-up** → After the coin lands, a pop-up shows the result:

- **Heads:** "Say the question out loud or drink! 🍻"
- **Tails:** "Safe! 😮‍💨"

**5. Next** → A **Next** button moves on to the next question.
